**NOUNS**
dextra, dextrae, f. – right hand
ignis, ignis, m. – fire
īra, īrae, f. – anger
Mūcius (Mūciī) Scaevola (Scaevolae), m. – Mucius Scaevola
nōmen, nōminis, n. – name
vim – accusative of vīs, meaning “force, strength”

**ADJECTIVES**
attonitus, attonita, attonitum – astounded
Etrūscus, Etrūsca, Etrūscum – Etruscan
noster, nostra, nostrum – our
occultus, occulta, occultum – hidden
parātus, parāta, parātum – prepared (often + infinitive)
similis, simile + genitive or dative – like
tantus, tanta, tantum – so great
tuus, tua, tuum – your, yours

**PRONOUNS**
eum – him (masculine accusative singular)
id – that (neuter accusative singular)
mē – me (accusative singular)
mihi – me (dative singular)
nōs – we (nominative plural; the textbook incorrectly labels this “accusative singular”)
quod – which
tē – you (accusative singular)
tibi – you (dative singular)

**VERBS**
cōnsūmō, cōnsūmere, cōnsūmpsī, cōnsūmptum – to consume
faciō, facere, fēcī, factum – to do, make
inquit – says or said (note that in classical Latin this verb is only used with direct speech)
occīdō, occīdere, occīdī, occīsum – to kill
ostendō, ostendere, ostendī, ostentum – to show
pōnō, pōnere, posuī, positum – to put, place

**ADVERBS**
ibi – there
statim – immediately
subitō – suddenly
ubi – where

**PREPOSITIONS**
prope + accusative – near

**CONJUNCTIONS**
igitur – therefore
nisi – if not, unless

**PHRASES**
gerere bellum – to wage war
