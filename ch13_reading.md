**NOUNS**
bracchium, bracchiī, n. – arm
iūdex, iūdicis, m. – judge
locus, locī, m. – place
Maecēnās, Maecēnātis, m. – Maecenas
nūgae, nūgārum, f. pl. – trifles
Vesta, Vestae, f. – Vesta
Via Sacra – a street in the Roman Forum

**ADJECTIVES**
alius, alia, aliud – another, other
dīves, dīvitis – rich
doctus, docta, doctum – learned
importūnus, importūna, importūnum – boorish
nōtus, nōta, nōtum – known
omnis, omne – each, every, all
piger, pigra, pigrum – lazy
sacer, sacra, sacrum – holy, sacred
suus, sua, suum – his, her, its, their

**PRONOUNS**
ēius – his
mēcum – with me (= cum mē)
nihil – nothing
quae (neuter plural accusative) – what, which
quid? – what
tēcum – with you (= cum tē)

**VERBS**
accurrō, accurrere, accurrī, accursum – to run up
audī! (second person singular) – hear!
discēdō, discēdere, discessī, discessum – to leave, withdraw, go away
dūcō, dūcere, dūxī, ductum – to lead
invīsō, invīsere, invīsī, invīsum – to visit
licet + dative + infinitive – it is allowed, it is permitted (for someone) (to do something)
nōlō (irregular verb) – not to want, to be unwilling
respondeō, respondēre, respondī, respōnsum – to answer
rogō, rogāre, rogāvī, rogātum – to ask
valē! (second person singular) – goodbye! (literally “be well!”)

**ADVERBS**
quō? – to what place?
tantum – only
tum – then

**PREPOSITIONS**
apud + accusative – at the house of
prō + ablative – for, on behalf of

**CONJUNCTIONS**
atque – and
enim – for, in fact

**PHRASES**
dulcissime rērum – dear fellow, literally “the sweetest of all things”
nōlī fugere! (second person singular) – do not run!
quid agis – how are you?
trāns Tiberim – on the other side of the Tiber river
ūnusquisque nostrum – each one of us
